Executive Summary & Core Recommendations

Australia faces compounding crises in regional food security, escalating household grocery costs, and severe farm enterprise debt stress. Orthodox macroeconomic policy assumes that inflation is universally caused by excess consumer demand and can be cooled by Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) cash rate increases.

This Policy Brief presents empirical econometric and econophysics evidence proving that central bank interest rate hikes operate as a powerful cost-push inflationary driver on non-discretionary subsistence goods (food, energy, water, shelter). By compounding agricultural working capital debt ($P_{Q6}$) while supermarket duopolies defend their ~28% gross margins against captive consumers, monetary tightening crushes regional primary producers while inflating retail shelf prices.

We propose three actionable legislative and structural reforms grounded in the Universal Price Equation (UPE) and Theorem 5 (Statutory Debt Bifurcation):

  • Statutory Debt Bifurcation for Essential Productive Capital: Legislate a distinct asset tier separating tangible agricultural and productive infrastructure debt from speculative credit, capping interest on productive capital at $r_{\text{compound}} \equiv 0.0%$ with deterministic linear principal amortization.
  • Chartering Regional Sovereign Mutual Credit Unions: Empower local government shires and regional credit unions under the Corporations Act 2001 (Mutual Societies provisions) to issue non-usurious liquidity pegged to verified physical difficulty ($WEST$ Tokens) and local commodity buffers ($TS_0$).
  • Duopoly Toll Extraction Caps on Subsistence Staples: Impose a statutory gross margin cap ($R_a \le 12%$) on essential caloric and hydration items, eliminating artificial retail shelf-slotting fees and establishing true difficulty parity ($DQ \approx 1.00$).

1. The Macroeconomic Paradox: Interest Rate Cost-Push Inflation

Orthodox monetary theory models price as a single-scalar equilibrium variable ($P = f(S, D)$), assuming that rate hikes suppress aggregate demand. In primary agriculture and food processing, however, production requires substantial working capital in advance (seed, diesel, fertilizer, electricity, machinery leases).

The Working Capital Cost Channel

Grounded in empirical macroeconomic literature (Barth & Ramey 2001; Ravenna & Walsh 2006; Tillmann 2008), higher borrowing costs directly increase upstream unit production costs:$$P_t = P_e + P_b + P_m(i \cdot D)$$

  • An RBA cash rate hike of +425 bps increased weekly interest expenses for a representative 10-farm broadacre cluster in NSW from $10,211/week to $24,923/week, rapidly draining $1.50M in Farm Management Deposits (FMDs) within 18 months.
  • Farmers cannot pass these interest hikes downstream due to monopsonistic duopoly wholesale gatekeeping ($DQ_{\text{farmgate}} \approx 0.13$).
  • Conversely, supermarket duopolies pass wholesale costs plus capital return hurdles directly to consumers, exploiting the collapse of consumer price elasticity on essential food ($\epsilon \to 0$ under Stone-Geary subsistence thresholds).
