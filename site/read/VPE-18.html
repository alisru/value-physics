Executive Legislative Summary & Preamble

By treating all debt as a homogenous financial asset yielding compound interest ($L(t) = L_0 e^{rt}$), central bank rate hikes transmit an aggressive cost-push inflationary impulse through upstream working capital ($P_{Q6}$), accelerating foreclosures, eroding regional capital, and transferring localized wealth to centralized financial monopolies.

This Statutory Model Charter & Sovereign Credit Union Bylaws provides an operational, legally defensible framework for regional credit unions, mutual financial institutions, and local government councils to deploy Theorem 5 (Statutory Debt Bifurcation) and the Value Physics Tri-Fold Ledger Architecture. Grounded in historical precedents (the Swiss WIR Bank established in 1934, the Regional Investment Corporation Act 2018, and standard Mutual Societies regulations under the Australian Corporations Act 2001), this charter establishes non-usurious, zero-compound-interest productive capital clearing ($r_{\text{compound}} \equiv 0.0%$) to permanently stabilize regional food, energy, water, shelter, and care systems.

Article I: Name, Jurisdictional Scope & Constitutional Purpose

Section 1.01 Name and Institutional Status

The entity established under this Charter shall operate as a Member-Owned Mutual Financial Institution and Sovereign Clearing Trust (hereafter, the "Sovereign Credit Union" or "SCU").

Section 1.02 Constitutional Purpose

The core objective of the SCU is to preserve the physical substrate and productive carrying capacity ($R_n$) of the regional community by:

  • Eliminating compound interest usury on essential productive capital.
  • Anchoring monetary credit emission directly to verified physical difficulty ($WEST$ Tokens) and thermodynamic exergy ($P_e$).
  • Maintaining the Invariant Physical Base Price ($P_H$) across the 5 essential physical needs (Food, Energy, Water, Shelter, and Care).
  • Insulating regional enterprises from external macroeconomic credit contraction and speculative financial shocks.

Article II: Statutory Debt Bifurcation (Theorem 5 Implementation)

Section 2.01 Mandatory Asset Class Bifurcation

All credit facilities, loans, and financial obligations issued or administered by the SCU must be legally classified into one of two mutually exclusive tiers:

  • Class A (Productive Substrate Capital): All loans allocated to tangible physical reproduction—including agricultural cropping land, grain storage silos, solar/hydro microgrids, water reservoirs and reticulation networks, modular residential shelter construction, and clinical medical equipment.
  • Class B (Discretionary Commercial Credit): Loans allocated to non-essential commercial ventures, luxury consumer acquisitions, or external market trade.

Section 2.02 Invariant 0.0% Compound Usury Rule (Class A)

All Class A productive capital facilities are subject to an absolute statutory prohibition on compound interest:$$r_{\text{compound}} \equiv 0.0%$$No capitalization of interest, penalty rate compounding, or rolling compound overdraft fees shall ever attach to Class A obligations.

Section 2.03 Linear Principal Amortization Schedule

Repayment of Class A facilities shall follow the deterministic linear amortization schedule formalized in Theorem 5:$$\text{Obligation}(t) = \frac{C_{\text{principal}}}{T_{\text{term}}}$$Where $C_{\text{principal}}$ is the exact statutory cost of the physical equipment/substrate, and $T_{\text{term}}$ is the agreed operational lifespan (e.g., 15 years for agricultural machinery, 25 years for solar microgrids, 50 years for rammed-earth housing).

Section 2.04 Concessional Reality Gap Absorber

In the event of an exogenous climatic disaster (e.g., severe multi-year drought with crop yield drop $\ge 35%$) or regional energy shock, debt amortization payments are automatically paused without penalty, extending $T_{\text{term}}$ commensurately.
